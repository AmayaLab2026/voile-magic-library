*National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Technical Translation Document NASA TT F-9285*

> **“I am writing this project in prison, a few days before my death. I believe in the feasibility of my idea, and this belief sustains me in my terrible predicament... If my idea, after being evaluated by scientific experts, is recognized as feasible, I will be happy with the thought that I have rendered a great service to my country and mankind. I can then calmly meet death, knowing that my idea will not perish with me, but will remain among humanity for whom I am ready to sacrifice my life.”**

## Scholarly Significance and Academic Status

Along humanity's arduous journey to break free from the shackles of gravity and reach into the vast ocean of stars, there exists a group of monumentally profound documents that were buried in historical archives for decades, yet were half a century ahead of their time in theoretical depth.

This collection has been thoroughly translated and edited based on the authoritative volume *Pioneers of Rocket Technology* (*Пионеры ракетной техники*), published in 1964 by Nauka Press, Moscow, under the auspices of the **Institute for the History of Natural Science and Technology, Academy of Sciences of the USSR**, as well as the technical translation archive completed by the **National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)** in 1965 (**NASA TT F-9285**).

This work fully preserves and systematically presents the original manuscripts and seminal works of two of the most legendary pioneering thinkers in the annals of astronautics:

1. **Nikolay Ivanovich Kibal'chich (N. I. Kibal'chich, 1853–1881)**: Narodnaya Volya revolutionary and explosives expert. Sentenced to death in 1881 for his involvement in the assassination of Tsar Alexander II. In the final days before his execution, inside the dark and freezing solitary confinement cell of the death fortress, he sketched on scrap paper and mathematically calculated the **first engineering proposal in human history for a manned aeronautical flying machine propelled by the controlled combustion of gunpowder rockets**.
2. **Yuriy Vasil'yevich Kondratyuk (Yu. V. Kondratyuk, 1897–1942)**: One of the twentieth century's most visionary independent scientists. Without a formal higher degree, working in grain elevators on the remote Siberian frontier during harsh years of isolation, he independently derived the fundamental equations of rocket mechanics with extraordinary mathematical insight, and for the first time in history proposed:
   - **The Lunar Orbit Rendezvous (LOR) Scheme**: Leaving the main spacecraft in lunar orbit while a lightweight lunar lander descends separately to the surface and returns to rendezvous and dock—a concept adopted wholesale by NASA half a century later as the **cornerstone of the Apollo Moon landing program** (John Houbolt, key decision-maker of the Apollo program, publicly paid tribute to Kondratyuk);
   - **Gravitational Slingshot (Gravity Assist)**: Utilizing the gravitational fields of the Moon and planets in orbital motion to accelerate or decelerate spacecraft;
   - **Aerodynamic Gliding Reentry with Atmospheric Braking** and the concept of **Multilayer Thermal Insulation (MLI)** heat shields;
   - **Staged Rocket Droppable Tanks** and propellant mass optimization theories;
   - **Large-scale Space Stations, Orbital Refueling Bases, and Solar Thermal Power Systems**.

## Editorial Principles and Typographical Standards

1. **Faithful Reproduction of Original Figures**: All 42 original engineering sketches, orbital vector charts, structural schematics, and manuscript facsimiles included in the original publications have been extracted and high-resolution processed from the NASA and Russian source archives, cross-referenced accurately with the text.
2. **Archival Precision and Modern Metrology**: Historical Imperial Russian units of measurement (such as pood, arshin, verst, line) and idiosyncratic terminology used by the early pioneers are rigorously preserved in context, accompanied by editorial conversions to the International System of Units (SI) and modern aerospace conventions.
3. **Rigorous KaTeX Formula Typesetting**: Mathematical expressions—including the Tsiolkovskiy–Kondratyuk characteristic velocity equations, atmospheric drag integrals, and gravitational potential calculus—have been completely typeset in high-grade KaTeX mathematical formatting.
