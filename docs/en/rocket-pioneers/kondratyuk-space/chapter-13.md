# Chapter 13 · Outline of Essential Experimental Research Tasks
### Section XIII. Outline of Essential Experimental Work
**By Yuriy Vasil'yevich Kondratyuk (Yu. V. Kondratyuk)**  
*(From NASA TT F-9285, pp. 128–130 / Original work, pp. 90–93)*

---

In view of our incomplete knowledge in certain fundamental scientific disciplines and the complete lack of practical engineering experience in constructing manned interplanetary rocket vehicles, it is imperative to execute a systematic, stepwise series of foundational scientific experiments and technological development programs prior to directly undertaking the fabrication of final manned interplanetary spacecraft:

---

## Task I: Investigation of Combustion Chambers and Nozzles in Various Media and in Vacuum

A comprehensive study of rocket engine combustion chambers and de Laval exhaust nozzles (exit tubes) operating in media of differing densities and backpressures:
* Determination of actual combustion efficiency, reaction temperatures, and heat-transfer rates along the inner walls of the chamber and nozzle;
* Investigation of effective cooling techniques for chambers and nozzles (specifically regenerative cooling);
* Determination of functional relationships among mass flow rate $dM/dt$, combustion chamber volume, nozzle throat area, and exit plane cross-section;
* Thorough investigation of gas expansion, thermodynamics, and thrust characteristics under near-zero backpressures and in high vacuum.

---

## Task II: Design of Proportional Passive Structures and Methods for Consuming Them as Fuel

In-depth structural design optimization of proportional passive structures for rocket stages (tanks, airframes, trusses, and plumbing):
* Development of lightweight, high-strength structural joints and interconnects;
* Exploration of mechanical and chemical methods to melt or pulverize spent structural components upon propellant exhaustion, feeding them into the combustion chamber as secondary metallic fuel to react with oxidizer.

---

## Task III: Industrial Production Technology for High-Energy Propellant Compounds

Laboratory synthesis and scaling to industrial production lines for specialized, ultra-high-energy rocket propellants:
* **Boranes: Boron trihydride / Diborane ($\text{BH}_3$ / $\text{B}_2\text{H}_6$)**;
* **Silane ($\text{SiH}_4$)**;
* **Liquid ozone ($\text{O}_3$)**;
* **Liquid acetylene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$)**;
* **Liquefied methane ($\text{CH}_4$)** and related high-energy chemical compounds.

---

## Task IV: Development of Manned Hermetic Cabins and Life-Support Instrumentation

Determination of optimal geometric configurations, hermetic seals, impact-absorbing suspension mountings, and ergonomic instrumentation layouts for manned pressurized crew compartments.

---

## Task V: Development of Automatic Flight Control, Attitude Stabilization, and Navigation Instruments

Design and fabrication of the complete suite of automatic flight controls, gyroscopic stabilizers, jet-vane servo actuators, and dual-range integrating apparent-weight accelerometers detailed in Chapter 11.

---

## Task VI: Physiological Acceleration Tolerance and Survival Limits in Low-Pressure, Oxygen-Rich Atmospheres

Systematic medical experiments utilizing large terrestrial centrifuges and hypobaric decompression chambers:
* Determination of human tolerance thresholds and endurance limits under linear and centrifugal accelerations in supine and various physiological orientations;
* Investigation of human physiological adaptation to prolonged habitation and work in low-pressure artificial environments (reduced to $0.2 \sim 0.3\ \text{atm}$) enriched with high concentrations of oxygen.

---

## Task VII: Rapid Autonomous Deep-Space Celestial Positioning and Ephemeris Reduction Methods

Development of specialized, lightweight celestial sighting instruments (space sextants and stellar trackers) operable by the crew within the cockpit, accompanied by rapid tabular and graphical celestial navigation reduction procedures, enabling astronauts to resolve inertial coordinates and osculating orbital elements relative to Earth with high precision within minutes.

---

## Task VIII: Sounding and Exploration of the Upper Atmosphere to 100 Kilometers

Exploration of the physical and chemical characteristics of the atmosphere from sea level to 100 km altitude utilizing sounding rockets and specialized gun-launched projectiles:
* High-precision measurement of density, pressure, and temperature profiles across diverse altitudes;
* Identification of chemical composition, molecular ionization states, and high-altitude wind currents.

---

## Task IX: Investigation of Aerodynamic Drag and Intense Surface Heating in the Dense Lower Atmosphere

Systematic analysis of drag laws and aerodynamic surface heating for high-velocity bodies traversing the dense lower atmosphere ($\rho \approx \rho_0$):
* For moderate velocities, firing specialized instrumented projectiles from high-velocity ordnance;
* For hypersonic speeds of several kilometers per second, employing large ground guns combined with rocket-boosted models to measure shock compression heating and drag coefficients in free flight.

---

## Task X: Hypersonic Heating in the Upper Atmosphere and Winged Gliding Landing Trials

Comprehensive experimental investigation of aerodynamic heating phenomena encountered by bodies moving at hypersonic velocities through the rarified upper atmosphere (empirically validating the theoretical derivations of Chapter 9):
* Experimental validation of the radiative thermal protection performance of refractory ceramic tiles (corundum alumina bricks, porcelain tiles) under extreme convective heat flux;
* Aerodynamic testing of self-balancing variable-incidence wings and control empennage across the entire speed envelope from hypersonic to subsonic regimes;
* Extensive flight trials using scaled unpowered and powered glide models—air-dropped from aircraft or rocket-boosted from the ground—to validate the complete aerodynamic landing architecture, including flare-out maneuvers and water ditching.
