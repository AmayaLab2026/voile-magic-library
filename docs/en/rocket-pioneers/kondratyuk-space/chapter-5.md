## Mathematical Definition of the Passive Deadweight Coefficient $q$

In rocket structural mechanics, any hardware mass that is not converted into propulsive exhaust to generate reactive thrust is designated as **passive weight** (structural deadweight).

We define the **relative structural deadweight coefficient $q_i$** of the $i$-th stage as the ratio of its discarded empty structural shell mass (including empty tanks, residual plumbing, and interstage adapters) to the total mass of active propellant contained within that stage:
$$q_i = \frac{M_{\text{empty}, i}}{M_{\text{propellant}, i}} = \frac{M_{\text{empty}, i}}{M_{io} - M_{if}}$$

Accounting for this structural deadweight coefficient, the payload mass ratio equation for a single stage of a multistage rocket is rigorously expressed by algebraic substitution:
$$M_{io} = M_{if} \cdot n_i = (M_{\text{payload}, i} + M_{\text{empty}, i}) \cdot n_i$$

Substituting $M_{\text{empty}, i} = q_i (M_{io} - M_{if}) = q_i M_{if} (n_i - 1)$ into the relation and solving for the stage's effective useful payload $M_{\text{payload}, i}$:
$$M_{\text{payload}, i} = M_{if} - q_i M_{if} (n_i - 1) = M_{if} \left[1 - q_i (n_i - 1)\right]$$

From this, we obtain the comprehensive mass magnification multiplier accounting for structural deadweight:
$$\frac{M_{io}}{M_{\text{payload}, i}} = \frac{n_i}{1 - q_i (n_i - 1)}$$

where $n_i = e^{\frac{W_i}{u_i}}$ is the ideal propellant load rating in the absence of deadweight.

---

## Critical Divergence Condition and Structural Safety Limits

Examining the denominator term $1 - q_i (n_i - 1)$ in the exact formulation above, a critical physical boundary condition becomes immediately evident:

$$1 - q_i (n_i - 1) > 0$$

that is:
$$q_i < \frac{1}{n_i - 1}$$

As long as the structural deadweight coefficient $q_i$ strictly satisfies $q_i \ll \frac{1}{n_i - 1}$, the total mass required for each stage remains only slightly higher than the ideal zero-deadweight state (cf. Editorial Commentary 6).

**However, once the deadweight coefficient $q_i$ approaches or exceeds the critical limit $\frac{1}{n_i - 1}$, the denominator approaches zero, and the required initial liftoff mass violently diverges toward mathematical infinity!**

This mathematical singularity reveals an unyielding law of astronautical structural engineering:  
When the characteristic velocity allocation $W_i$ of a single stage is large (implying a large $n_i$ and an exceedingly small critical threshold $\frac{1}{n_i - 1}$), the lightweight requirements imposed on tanks and propulsion structures become extraordinarily unforgiving; **even a one percent increase in structural deadweight unleashes a catastrophic multiplier surge in total liftoff mass**.

## Quantitative Engineering Breakdown of Tanks, Engines, and Structural Airframes

To suppress the deadweight coefficient $q$ safely below this critical threshold, all major hardware subsystems must undergo rigorous structural mass minimization:

### 1. Propellant Tank Lightweighting
* Propellant tanks primarily sustain internal autogenous vapor pressurization loads and hydrostatic fluid pressure generated by flight acceleration;
* Utilization of premium lightweight alloys with high yield strength and ultimate tensile properties (such as high-strength duralumin alloys and thin-walled nickel-chromium stainless steels);
* Tank wall thickness is minimized based on momentless membrane tension theory, eliminating stress concentrations and redundant gauge thicknesses;
* Under Kondratyuk's design of nested conical thin shells and tension-rod suspension, modern thin-walled propellant tanks can realistically achieve:
  $$q_{\text{tank}} \approx 0.04 \sim 0.07$$

### 2. Liquid Rocket Engine and Powerplant Systems
* Elimination of thick-walled cast structures; thrust chambers and nozzles are formed from lightweight thin-walled liquid-cooled tubular jackets brazed together;
* High-speed turbopumps and high-pressure manifolding adopt a compact, coaxial integrated layout;
* The dry weight of the engine typically amounts to $1\% \sim 2\%$ of the stage's total thrust, contributing merely:
  $$q_{\text{engine}} \approx 0.01 \sim 0.03$$

### 3. Comprehensive Stage Structural Deadweight
Aggregating propellant tanks, engines, interstage separation rings, high-pressure helium pressurization bottles, avionics, and plumbing, the composite structural deadweight coefficient for each stage in modern aerospace practice can be reliably contained within:
$$q \approx 0.06 \sim 0.10$$

## Full-Mission Interplanetary Flight Numerical Example

To demonstrate the concrete feasibility of this architecture to the scientific community, Kondratyuk presented a compelling full-mission numerical case study:

Assume the total characteristic velocity required for a complete mission is:
$$W = 14,460\ \text{m/s}$$
*(Note: This velocity budget is sufficient not only to achieve escape velocity from Earth's gravity field into deep space, but includes ample reserves for midcourse corrections, orbital insertion into a target planetary orbit, and the return departure impulse.)*

Assuming high-energy hydrocarbons or liquid hydrogen/liquid oxygen propellants with an effective exhaust velocity of:
$$u = 4,000\ \text{m/s}$$

The ideal total load rating without deadweight is:
$$n_{\text{ideal}} = e^{\frac{W}{u}} = e^{\frac{14460}{4000}} = e^{3.615} \approx 37.15$$

Adopting a four-stage tandem configuration ($z = 4$) with characteristic velocity distributed equally among stages, and assuming a realistic and conservative structural deadweight coefficient $q = 0.08$:
* Applying the recursive mass multiplier formula yields an overall mission liftoff mass ratio of approximately:
  $$\frac{M_0}{M_{\text{payload}}} \approx 80 \sim 100$$
* This indicates that: **A medium-sized multistage rocket with a total liftoff mass on the order of 100 to 200 metric tons is entirely sufficient to propel a 1- to 2-ton crewed spacecraft—carrying cosmonauts, life support systems, and scientific instrumentation—to lunar orbit and return them safely to Earth!**

This transparent mathematical calculation permanently rescued interplanetary travel from the realm of speculative science fiction, anchoring it firmly within the domain of rigorous mechanics and achievable modern industrial manufacturing!
