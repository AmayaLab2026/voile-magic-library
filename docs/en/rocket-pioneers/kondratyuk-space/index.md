## Historical Significance of a Monumental Work

*Conquest of Interplanetary Space* (*Zavoyevaniye mezhplanetnykh prostranstv*, Russian: *Завоевание межпланетных пространств*) stands as an enduring monument in the history of aerospace engineering and the theory of interplanetary flight. Published privately by Yuriy Vasil'yevich Kondratyuk at his own expense in Novosibirsk in early 1929, the print run comprised merely a few thousand copies.

In this concise yet seminal monograph, distilling over a decade of painstaking individual research, Kondratyuk combined rigorous analytical mechanics with profound engineering intuition to solve—for the first time in human history—**the complete flight dynamics and mission trajectory architecture** required to depart Earth, land on the Moon, and return safely:

* **Lunar Orbit Rendezvous (LOR)**: In Chapters 7 and 10, Kondratyuk demonstrated that reaching the lunar surface does not require landing the entire massive interplanetary spacecraft. Instead, the main craft should remain in a lunar parking orbit, while a lightweight dedicated landing craft (excursion module) descends to the Moon, carries out exploration, and ascends to rendezvous with the orbital mothership. Half a century later, this trajectory architecture was adopted in its entirety by the United States Apollo Program and publicly acknowledged by NASA Chief Engineer John Houbolt as the **"Kondratyuk Route"**.
* **Aerodynamic Deceleration and Gliding Reentry**: In Chapter 9, Kondratyuk formulated the mathematical system governing hypersonic lifting reentry into a dense planetary atmosphere, analyzing kinetic energy dissipation, stagnation heat flux, and radiative thermal equilibrium.
* **Thermochemical Analysis of High-Energy Chemical Propellants**: He calculated theoretical combustion enthalpies, exhaust velocities, and optimal mixture ratios for dozens of propellant combinations, including liquid oxygen/liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen/methane, and fuels incorporating high-energy light metals (lithium, beryllium, boron) and their hydrides.
* **Continuous Infinite Staging Limit Theory**: He independently derived recursive mass equations incorporating structural deadweight ratios, formulating the mathematical theory of continuous infinite staging.

## Author's Preface
*(From NASA TT F-9285, pp. 60–61 / Original work, pp. 3–4)*

The principal core of this work was written in 1916 and has subsequently undergone three thorough expansions and fundamental revisions.

The author fervently hopes that he has succeeded in presenting the majestic enterprise of conquering the Solar System not merely as an abstract theoretical principle (whose practical engineering implementation might be relegated to some distant era of future science and technology), but **as an actionable strategic combat plan with concrete operational routes**. Even if not exhaustive in every detail, this plan is supported throughout by definite physical data entirely realizable within contemporary engineering and technology, requiring only preliminary ground tests that present no exceptional practical difficulties.

Moreover, from the initial preparatory ground trials to the ultimate realization of a lunar voyage, the material resources and capital expenditure demanded by this entire engineering undertaking—on any pre-evaluable scale—**are less than the outlays required by our nation to construct a few modern battleships of the largest tonnage!**

As for the pioneering works of Engineer K. E. Tsiolkovskiy in this same discipline, the author learned of their existence only after completing the body of his manuscript, and only recently had the opportunity to peruse portions of Tsiolkovskiy's extensive paper *"Investigation of Cosmic Space by Reactive Devices"*, published in the 1911 issue of *Vestnik Vozdukhoplavaniya* (*Aeronautical Herald*). I hereby solemnly affirm the indisputable historical priority of Engineer Tsiolkovskiy in resolving many of the fundamental cornerstones of this field.

Nevertheless, those passages in the present work treating concepts that are clearly not entirely new have not been excised: on the one hand, to maintain the organic integrity and internal rigor of the monograph without compelling interested readers to search for long-out-of-print back issues of *Vestnik Vozdukhoplavaniya*; on the other hand, because the distinct approach and mathematical formulations employed here provide a fresh perspective and new insight into the entire subject of astronautical dynamics.

Likewise, the author had no opportunity to consult any foreign works by Western colleagues in this field, nor even to read the continuation of Engineer Tsiolkovskiy's work published in Part 2 of that journal in 1912.

Many mechanical formulas and nearly all numerical values cited in this book have been simplified and rounded off, and in certain instances rather coarse approximations have been adopted. This has been done because we still acutely lack the body of ground test data indispensable for highly refined calculations; in our present circumstance, where even one percent accuracy cannot yet be guaranteed, dwelling on hundredths of a percent is of no practical utility. The sole intent of certain elaborate calculations herein is merely to illustrate to the reader **the order of magnitude of the physical quantities involved and their general functional variation along the flight trajectory**; prior to the completion of corresponding ground experiments, absolutely rigorous engineering figures cannot physically be established.

For precisely the same reason of prudence, this volume includes no detailed machine shop assembly blueprints; general structural and configuration principles are readily set forth, but detailed designs cannot be fabricated out of thin air in the absence of test-stand data. Any speculative drawings purporting to give specific dimensions would only confuse and mislead future constructors rather than assist them.

Finally, the author takes this opportunity to express his most sincere and profound gratitude to the distinguished master of mechanics and aeronautical engineering, **Professor Vladimir Petrovich Vetchinkin (V. P. Vetchinkin)**. It was through Professor Vetchinkin's invaluable scientific guidance, critical annotations, and selfless support toward publication that this monograph was able to overcome numerous obstacles and finally reach the reading public.

**Yuriy Kondratyuk**  
*Novosibirsk, 1929*

## Foreword by Scientific Advisor Prof. V. P. Vetchinkin
*(From NASA TT F-9285, pp. 61–62)*

This work by Yuriy Vasil'yevich Kondratyuk represents, following the fundamental pioneering work of Professor K. E. Tsiolkovskiy, **the most complete, most rigorous, and most profoundly original monograph** in the Russian and Soviet literature on the problem of reactive flight!

Working in isolation without prior knowledge of the treatises of Tsiolkovskiy or foreign authors, the author, through independent investigation, not only rediscovered the fundamental equations of rocket propulsion and mass ratios, but substantially surpassed previous investigations in scope and depth across numerous critical domains:
* The author carries out an admirable quantitative mechanics integration of active boost trajectories, optimum climb angles, and atmospheric drag losses;
* He pioneers the aerodynamic deceleration theory of using winged supersonic gliding for atmospheric reentry braking;
* He puts forward the revolutionary Lunar Orbit Rendezvous (LOR) architecture: establishing a lunar parking orbit, detaching a small landing craft to touch down on the Moon, and ascending to rendezvous with the orbital mothership;
* He presents detailed calculations on high-energy light metal fuels and space solar concentrators.

This volume is not only an outstanding contribution to the theoretical literature of astrodynamics, but provides future practical designers with an actionable, operational roadmap. I recommend this book with the greatest enthusiasm to all scientists, engineers, and young scholars devoted to the noble cause of conquering the cosmos!

**Prof. V. P. Vetchinkin**  
*Head of the Department of Flight Dynamics, Moscow Aviation Institute / Chief Scientist, Central Aerohydrodynamic Institute (TsAGI)*
