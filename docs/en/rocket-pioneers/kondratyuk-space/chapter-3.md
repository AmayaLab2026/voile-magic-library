## Relationship Between Exhaust Velocity and Thermochemical Energy Conversion

From the rocket load rating formula $n_i = e^{\frac{W_i}{u_i}}$, it is immediately apparent that as the required characteristic velocity $W_i$ increases, the load rating expands exponentially. When the ratio $\frac{W_i}{u_i} > 1$, the mass ratio surges with extreme rapidity toward values that become nearly unbearable from an engineering standpoint.

Consequently, **maximizing the effective exhaust velocity $u$ of the propulsive working fluid is the paramount technological priority for checking the runaway growth of initial liftoff mass!**

The chemical reaction thermal energy released by the propellants within the engine combustion chamber is converted during nozzle expansion into directed kinetic energy of the high-speed gas stream. Let $Q$ denote the theoretical chemical reaction heat liberated per unit mass of propellant (measured in kilocalories per kilogram, $\text{kcal/kg}$).

According to the mechanical equivalent of heat ($1\ \text{kcal} = 427\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m} = 4186\ \text{J}$), if this chemical heat could be converted with an ideal thermal efficiency $\eta$ into axial kinetic energy of the exhaust stream without loss, the thermodynamic formula for the theoretical effective exhaust velocity $u$ would be:
$$u = \sqrt{2 \cdot g \cdot 427 \cdot Q \cdot \eta} \approx \sqrt{8372 \cdot Q \cdot \eta} \quad (\text{m/s})$$

When accounting for real-world engineering imperfections—such as incomplete adiabatic expansion in actual nozzles, high-temperature dissociation and endothermic losses, convective heat transfer into nozzle walls, and nozzle divergence angle losses—the actual exhaust velocity typically attains $75\% \sim 87\%$ of the theoretical extreme (corresponding to an effective expansion efficiency of $\eta \approx 0.6 \sim 0.75$).

## Comprehensive Survey of Propellant Reaction Heats and Theoretical Exhaust Velocities

Kondratyuk systematically compiled and calculated the thermochemical properties of dozens of potential high-energy fuel and oxidizer combinations known to physical chemistry at the time, producing the world's first comprehensive **panoramic comparison table of high-energy rocket propellants**:

### 1. Conventional Hydrocarbons and Light Hydrocarbons (Reacting with Liquid Oxygen / Liquid Ozone)
* **Petroleum / Kerosene + Liquid Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**:
  * Primary reaction products: $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
  * Heat of combustion: $Q \approx 2.2 \sim 2.3\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical exhaust velocity: $u \approx 4250 \sim 4400\ \text{m/s}$
  * Expected practical velocity: $u_{\text{real}} \approx 3200 \sim 3500\ \text{m/s}$
* **Methane ($\text{CH}_4$) + Liquid Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**:
  * Primary reaction products: $\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
  * Heat of combustion: $Q \approx 2.5\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical exhaust velocity: $u \approx 4570\ \text{m/s}$
  * Expected practical velocity: $u_{\text{real}} \approx 3500 \sim 3700\ \text{m/s}$
* **Acetylene ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$) + Liquid Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**:
  * Endothermic compound characteristics; heat of combustion: $Q \approx 2.9\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical exhaust velocity: $u \approx 4900\ \text{m/s}$
  * Disadvantage: Marked chemical instability and explosive decomposition tendencies under high liquid storage pressures.

### 2. Liquid Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) with Liquid Oxygen / Liquid Ozone Systems
* **Pure Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) + Liquid Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)**:
  * Reaction product: Superheated steam ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)
  * Theoretical heat of combustion: $Q \approx 3.24\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical limit exhaust velocity: $u_{\text{theor}} \approx 5200\ \text{m/s}$
  * Expected practical velocity: $u_{\text{real}} \approx 4000 \sim 4400\ \text{m/s}$
* **Pure Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) + Liquid Ozone ($\text{O}_3$)**:
  * Theoretical heat of combustion: $Q \approx 3.9\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical limit exhaust velocity: $u_{\text{theor}} \approx 5700\ \text{m/s}$
  * Expected practical velocity: $u_{\text{real}} \approx 4500 \sim 4800\ \text{m/s}$

### 3. High-Energy Light Metals and Their Hydrides (Metalized Fuels)
* **Metallic Lithium ($\text{Li}$) and Lithium Hydride ($\text{LiH}$) + Liquid Oxygen**:
  * Combustion products: $\text{Li}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
  * Extremely high combustion heat: $Q \approx 4.6 \sim 5.1\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical exhaust velocity exceeding $6000\ \text{m/s}$! However, lithium is scarce in the Earth's crust and prohibitively expensive.
* **Metallic Boron ($\text{B}$) and Diborane ($\text{B}_2\text{H}_6$) + Liquid Oxygen / Ozone**:
  * Combustion products: High-temperature boron trioxide ($\text{B}_2\text{O}_3$) and steam
  * Combustion heat: $Q \approx 4.2 \sim 5.0\ \text{kcal/g}$
  * Theoretical limit exhaust velocity: $u_{\text{theor}} \approx 5800 \sim 6400\ \text{m/s}$
  * Expected practical velocity reaching: $u_{\text{real}} \approx 4500 \sim 5000\ \text{m/s}$!
* **Metallic Beryllium ($\text{Be}$), Aluminum ($\text{Al}$), and Silane ($\text{SiH}_4$)**:
  * Aluminum oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) and beryllium oxide ($\text{BeO}$) exhibit exceptionally high enthalpies of formation ($Q \approx 3.4 \sim 4.4\ \text{kcal/g}$).

## Practical Engineering Feasibility Ranking of Propellants

Synthesizing this thermochemical data with critical real-world factors—including **natural terrestrial abundance, industrial refining costs, bulk liquid density, and technological maturity**—Kondratyuk established an authoritative developmental ranking for the practical adoption of rocket propellants:

1. **First Tier: Petroleum / Refined Kerosene + Liquid Oxygen**  
   The prime working fluid for the initial era of spaceflight. Its high physical density (liquid at room temperature) minimizes tank structural deadweight, combustion chamber operation is stable and dependable, and technical barriers are minimal, rendering it fully capable of executing early low-Earth orbital missions and suborbital flight testing.
2. **Second Tier: Methane ($\text{CH}_4$) + Liquid Oxygen**  
   The boiling point of methane ($-161^\circ\text{C}$) lies very close to that of liquid oxygen; its relatively high hydrogen-to-carbon ratio combines superior heat of combustion with favorable regenerative cooling characteristics, serving as an exceptional engineering bridge between kerosene and liquid hydrogen.
3. **Third Tier: Liquid Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) + Liquid Oxygen**  
   Occupies the dominant position for deep-space exploration and heavy interplanetary voyages. While its cryogenic boiling point and low density impose rigorous thermal insulation requirements on propellant tanks, its unmatched high specific impulse makes it the indispensable ultimate high-energy chemical fuel.
4. **Fourth Tier: Metallic Boron and Its Hydrides/Suspensions + Liquid Oxygen / Ozone**  
   Boron compounds possess extraordinary specific energy. If multiphase flow losses and nozzle slagging from solid particles can be surmounted, boron will yield immense strategic value for terminal upper stages requiring extreme thrust density.
