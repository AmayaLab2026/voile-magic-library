# Chapter 10 · Interplanetary Bases and the Rocket-Artillery Device
### Section X. Interplanetary Bases. Rocket-Artillery Device
**By Yuriy Vasil'yevich Kondratyuk (Yu. V. Kondratyuk)**  
*(From NASA TT F-9285, pp. 115–120 / Original work, pp. 73–80)*

---

For velocities less than half the exhaust velocity $u$ of the chosen chemical fuel (i.e., up to approximately $2500\ \text{m/s}$, disregarding for the moment the petroleum-air propulsion combination discussed on page 64), artillery launch methods are far more economical than pure rocket propulsion from the standpoint of both fuel expenditure and passive structural consumption. However, the human physiological constitution cannot withstand the immense accelerations of several thousand meters per second squared generated during gun launches.

It is therefore exceptionally rational to separate cargo and passive loads capable of sustaining accelerations of several thousand $\text{m/s}^2$ (with proper shock-absorbing packaging, virtually all supplies except the most delicate instruments can easily sustain such loads) from manned spacecraft, launching them into interplanetary space via a **"rocket-artillery composite device"**. Employing a gun-boosted launch for space freight can conserve up to 50% of the required active rocket propellants.

The primary technical hurdle of this scheme lies in searching for and capturing relatively small rocket freight containers in the vastness of space[^note_telescope]. Looking forward to an era when interplanetary flights will be conducted with regular frequency, we propose the following organizational architecture to secure immense material and economic dividends.

[^note_telescope]: Original Note 8: Regrettably, the author lacked reference manuals regarding the light-gathering and resolving powers of modern astronomical telescopes; it should be noted that the discussion of optical signal tracking and rendezvous for the "rocket-artillery device" was formulated on the basis of data that were not entirely reliable at the time.

## I. Lunar Satellite Intermediate Base (Artificial Interplanetary Base)

A rocket of immense mass is launched from Earth, carrying a substantial reserve of useful cargo, accelerating to a total effective velocity of approximately $W = 12,000\ \text{m/s}$. Because this rocket is designed without the requirement to carry fuel for returning to Earth or for propulsive deceleration, its final orbital mass $M_f$ will be **increased by a factor of $\eta$** compared to a rocket of identical initial launch mass $M_0$ that must execute a round-trip voyage.

This rocket is guided into an orbit around the Moon, becoming an **artificial satellite of the Moon** (the orbital radius should be as large as possible without permitting Earth's gravitational perturbations to pull it away). Once in orbit, it deploys an immense, highly reflective signal surface (for instance, an ultralight metallic reflective film spanning $10,000\ \text{m}^2$, measuring $100\ \text{m} \times 100\ \text{m}$). In space, such a planar mirror will be readily resolved and precisely tracked by large terrestrial telescopes.

This permanently stationed lunar orbital station constitutes the **"Interplanetary Intermediate Base"**.

Once this base is established, all subsequent deep-space expeditions departing from the base to any celestial body in the Solar System and returning to it will see their required propellant mass and initial launch mass **staggeringly reduced to $1/\eta$** of the quantities needed for direct round trips from Earth's surface!

The core operational function of the interplanetary base is to serve as a deep-space logistics transshipment hub and propellant refueling depot. To enable long-term operational sustainability, specialized orbital habitat and engineering structures must be designed:
1. **Tetrahedral space truss with immense moment of inertia**: Constructed from four lightweight structural trusses, tens or hundreds of meters in length, interconnected by high-tensile guy cables across four primary nodes to form a colossal **regular tetrahedral space frame**. In contrast to conventional cylindrical hulls, this three-dimensional tetrahedral geometry possesses an immense moment of inertia about any principal axis, imparting exceptional passive attitude stability in the disturbance-free environment of deep space;
2. **Centrifugal artificial gravity habitat**: If prolonged exposure to weightlessness proves detrimental to astronaut health, the astronomical observatory can remain fixed to the tetrahedral truss, while the crew living quarters are connected by a flexible steel cable tens of meters long to a counterweight (such as cargo reserves or water tanks). Rotating this tethered pair uniformly about their common center of mass generates steady centrifugal acceleration within the living cabin, providing astronauts with artificial gravity identical to that on Earth;
3. **Low-pressure oxygen-rich atmosphere**: To maximize living volume for a given pressure hull mass, internal atmospheric pressure should be lowered to the physiological minimum for safe respiration (e.g., $0.2 \sim 0.3\ \text{atm}$ of pure oxygen or oxygen-enriched gas), thereby drastically reducing structural tensile stresses on the cabin skin;
4. **Large solar concentrator mirrors**: Ultrathin, highly reflective metallic foils stretched over lightweight duralumin structural frames[^note_reflector], forming concave or planar mirror arrays spanning thousands of square meters. These mirrors focus solar radiation onto boilers to generate high-pressure steam driving turbogenerators, or directly power thermoelectric conversion elements, supplying inexhaustible electrical power;
5. **Direct optical heliographic communication with Earth**: Utilizing large, precision flat mirrors to direct reflected beams of sunlight toward observatories on the sunlit hemisphere of Earth, enabling robust, low-cost, high-reliability bidirectional deep-space communication using Morse code or pulsed optical signals.

[^note_reflector]: Original Note 9: A rational engineering structure for the mirrors consists of ultrathin reflective metal sheets stretched across lightweight duralumin frames.

## II. Rocket-Artillery Composite Launch and Orbital Rendezvous/Resupply Sequence

The operational sequence for delivering logistical consumables (fuel, oxygen, water, provisions, and structural components) to the lunar orbit intermediate base proceeds as follows:

1. **Launch timing and ballistic computation**: Based on precise ephemeris data for the base, an unmanned rocket cargo vehicle is launched at a predetermined instant from a specialized ultra-long terrestrial gun;
2. **Active boost and coasting trajectory insertion**: The cargo projectile exits the dense lower atmosphere with a muzzle velocity of approximately $2500\ \text{m/s}$, after which its onboard rocket motor ignites, accelerating the payload toward the rendezvous zone in lunar orbit;
3. **Telescopic tracking and optical beaconing**: As the cargo craft approaches the base sector, it deploys a compact reflector or fires a magnesium flare beacon, allowing base telescopes to lock onto its trajectory;
4. **Space tug interception and capture**: A small maneuverable rocket (space tug) stationed at the base sorties to intercept the incoming cargo craft. During rendezvous, the tug matches velocities, docks mechanically, and tows the container to berth at the interplanetary base, utilizing any residual propellant remaining in the cargo vehicle for orbital maneuvers if desired.

## III. Engineering Parameters of the Inclined Mountain Tunnel Gun

Because the rocket freight vehicle necessarily houses propellant valving, ignition systems, and instrumentation, these components in their assembled state cannot survive extreme artillery shocks of tens of thousands of $\text{m/s}^2$. Consequently, the barrel of the launch cannon must possess considerable length—**approximately 2 kilometers (2 km)**!

Such an immense launch barrel can be constructed by boring an inclined tunnel inside a mountain slope:
* **Tunnel barrel length**: $L \approx 2000\ \text{m}$;
* **Muzzle velocity**: $v_0 \approx 2500\ \text{m/s}$;
* **Mean launch acceleration**: Computed via kinematics, $a = v_0^2 / (2L)$:

$$a = \frac{2500^2}{2 \times 2000} \approx 1560\ \text{m/s}^2 \approx 160\, g$$

* **Launch duration**: $t = 2L / v_0 \approx 1.6\ \text{s}$.

Under an acceleration of $160\, g$, all solid structures, propellant tanks, well-packaged dry cargo, and ruggedized solenoid valves and relays can withstand the launch environment without damage.

Compared to conventional short-barrel military artillery, the peak gas pressure of the propelling powder charge within this ultra-long tunnel is substantially more moderate. Furthermore, owing to the large bore diameter, minor gas leakage through the designed $1 \sim 2\ \text{mm}$ annular clearance between the projectile and the tunnel wall constitutes an entirely negligible fraction of the vast volume of expanding combustion gases, exerting no detrimental effect on internal ballistic efficiency.
