# Section 1: Meshcherskiy's Mechanics of Variable Mass and Tsiolkovskiy's Theory of Astronautics

## I. I. V. Meshcherskiy and the Foundations of Dynamics of a Point of Variable Mass

The solid mechanical cornerstone underpinning Russia's world leadership in rocket dynamics was laid by the eminent mechanician and Professor of Applied Mechanics at the St. Petersburg Polytechnic Institute, **Ivan Vsevolodovich Meshcherskiy (1859–1935)**.

Prior to the close of the nineteenth century, classical theoretical mechanics (Newtonian mechanics) was almost exclusively confined to the study of material points or rigid-body systems of constant mass. However, during rocket acceleration driven by the expulsion of incandescent combustion gases, the vehicle's mass undergoes continuous, severe variation over time; similarly, the motion of comets, meteors, and raindrops falling through a resisting medium constitutes motion of bodies of variable mass.

In 1897, Meshcherskiy defended his landmark master's dissertation at St. Petersburg University, entitled *Dynamics of a Point of Variable Mass* (*Динамика точки переменной массы*, published by the St. Petersburg Imperial Academy of Sciences in 1897).

In this pioneering monograph, Meshcherskiy formulated the most general differential equation of motion for a point of variable mass (celebrated in mechanics as **Meshcherskiy's Fundamental Equation**):

$$M \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \vec{F} + \frac{dM}{dt} (\vec{u} - \vec{v})$$

where:
* $M(t)$ is the instantaneous mass of the point;
* $\vec{v}$ is the instantaneous velocity of the point in an inertial reference frame;
* $\vec{F}$ is the resultant of external forces acting on the point (e.g., gravity, aerodynamic drag);
* $dM/dt$ is the time rate of change of mass (for the mass discharge of a rocket, $dM/dt < 0$);
* $\vec{u}$ is the absolute velocity in the inertial frame of the microscopic mass elements separating from or adhering to the point;
* The vector difference $(\vec{u} - \vec{v})$ is the relative exhaust velocity $\vec{v}_r$ of the discharged mass with respect to the main body. The second term on the right-hand side:

$$\vec{R} = \frac{dM}{dt} \vec{v}_r$$

is precisely the **reactive thrust** exerted upon the rocket vehicle by the momentum expulsion of the working fluid!

In 1904, Meshcherskiy published a continuation paper in the *Transactions of the St. Petersburg Polytechnic Institute* entitled *Equations of Motion of a Point of Variable Mass in the General Case* (*Уравнения движения точки переменной массы в общем случае*). He generalized this theory to composite dynamic systems involving simultaneous mass accretion and shedding, systematically solving for vertical and inclined trajectories of variable-mass points in a uniform gravitational field, in resisting media, and in central gravitational fields.

Meshcherskiy's classical mathematical derivations furnished Tsiolkovskiy and all subsequent astronautical pioneers with an unassailable theoretical mechanics foundation. Meshcherskiy is universally honored as the **founder of the dynamics of bodies of variable mass**.

## II. K. E. Tsiolkovskiy and the Creation of Modern Astronautics

While Meshcherskiy formulated the rigorous abstract framework of theoretical mechanics, the monumental achievement of applying these dynamic principles to humanity's conquest of outer space—and crafting a comprehensive engineering blueprint for doing so—belongs undisputed to the father of astronautics, **Constantine Eduardovich Tsiolkovskiy (1857–1935)**.

### 1. *Investigation of Cosmic Space by Means of Reaction Devices* (1903 Foundational Treatise)
Tsiolkovskiy's rigorous mathematical investigations into rocket motion began in May 1897. In a worn manuscript notebook in Kaluga, he completely independently formulated and integrated the differential equations of rocket motion in a force-free vacuum.

In 1898, he completed his historical milestone manuscript, *Investigation of Cosmic Space by Means of Reaction Devices* (*Исследование мировых пространств реактивными приборами*). Following peer review delays in Russian academic journals, the paper was published in 1903 in issue No. 5 of *Nauchnoye Obozreniye* (*Scientific Review*) in St. Petersburg.

In this treatise, Tsiolkovskiy derived the world-famous **Tsiolkovskiy Rocket Equation**:

$$V = u \ln \left(1 + \frac{M_p}{M_0}\right) = u \ln \frac{M_{\text{takeoff}}}{M_{\text{dry}}}$$

This formula proclaims with extraordinary mathematical elegance that the ultimate ideal burnout velocity attainable by a rocket depends solely on the effective exhaust velocity $u$ of the propellant relative to the rocket, and the ratio of takeoff mass to structural dry mass (the mass ratio). To reach the second cosmic velocity (escape velocity, $11.2\ \text{km/s}$) required to break free from Earth's gravity, one must drastically maximize the effective exhaust velocity while minimizing structural deadweight.

Governed by this dynamic law, Tsiolkovskiy firmly demonstrated that: **traditional solid black powder rockets, possessing low specific impulse, are physically incapable of achieving manned cosmic flight!** The only viable path lay in utilizing high-energy-density **liquid propellants**!

In his 1903 paper, Tsiolkovskiy presented the world's first complete **engineering design for a liquid-propellant interplanetary rocket**:
* **Vehicle Aerodynamic Profile**: An elongated, streamlined metallic spindle-shaped hull;
* **Propellant Combination**: Pioneer selection of **liquid hydrogen (fuel) and liquid oxygen (oxidizer)** as high-energy reacting propellants;
* **Propulsion System Architecture**: Separate insulated tanks for liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen, with cryogenic piping feeding both propellants into the combustion chamber;
* **Nozzle and Cooling**: The combustion chamber terminated in an expansive, flared conical supersonic nozzle, accompanied by the visionary concept of **regenerative cooling**, routing cold liquid hydrogen through a double-walled outer jacket before injection into the chamber;
* **Attitude Control**: Graphite jet vanes positioned within the supersonic exhaust plume, supplemented by internal gyroscopic flywheels for precision autonomous attitude control in vacuum;
* **Manned Life-Support System**: A pressurized hermetic cabin in the rocket head, equipped with observation ports and periscopes, containing chemical absorption systems to continuously scrub carbon dioxide and moisture while supplying pure oxygen and regulating cabin atmosphere.

### 2. Evolution of Tsiolkovskiy's Subsequent Classical Works
Following the 1903 treatise, Tsiolkovskiy, enduring harsh living conditions and isolation, pursued a relentless expansion of astronautical theory:

* **1911–1912 Second Part**: Serialized in *Vestnik Vozdukhoplavaniya* (*Aeronautical Herald*, 1911 Nos. 19–22, 1912 Nos. 2–9). Tsiolkovskiy thoroughly investigated the gravitational energy expenditure required to escape Earth, optimal powered ascent trajectories accounting for atmospheric drag, solar radiation pressure effects (solar sailing), physiological consequences of complete weightlessness on human organisms, and concepts for permanent orbital space stations.
* **1914 Supplement**: Published at his own expense in Kaluga, *Investigation of Cosmic Space by Means of Reaction Devices (Supplement)* added visionary predictions regarding atomic propulsion and concentrated solar thermal mirrors.
* **1926 Revised Edition**: Comprehensively revised and expanded *Investigation of Cosmic Space*, offering a concrete technological roadmap for aerospace implementation.
* **1929 *Cosmic Rocket Trains* (*Многоступенчатые ракеты*)**: Here Tsiolkovskiy formulated the world's first **rigorous mathematical dynamics of multistage rocketry (tandem series staging and parallel clustering)**. He proved that while single-stage chemical rockets were limited by structural mass ratios from overcoming Earth's gravity, multistage "Rocket Trains" shedding empty tanks at each stage could effortlessly attain first and second cosmic velocities!
* **1935 *Maximum Speed of a Rocket***: In one of his final scientific treatises, Tsiolkovskiy developed the mathematical optimization theory of propellant cross-feeding and simultaneous firing for clustered parallel rocket engine systems.

Tsiolkovskiy's body of work erected the grand edifice of modern astronautics. His immortal motto: "The Earth is the cradle of mankind, but mankind cannot live in the cradle forever," remains the eternal beacon for space exploration worldwide.
