# Section 4: Early Soviet Rocket Research Groups and Complete Historical Bibliography

## I. From Theoretical Pioneers to National Scientific Collectives (GDL, GIRD, and RNII)

In the decades prior to the October Revolution, although Russian scientists consistently stood at the forefront of global theoretical inquiries into rocket propulsion, the harsh realities of Tsarist rule, the devastating Civil War, foreign intervention, and the arduous postwar economic recovery deprived the pioneers of the material foundation and industrial base necessary to translate scientific theories into large-scale engineering reality.

Objectively speaking, with the exception of F. A. Tsander's small-scale laboratory engine firings in the late 1920s, the first generation of Russian rocket pioneers (Tsiolkovskiy, Kondratyuk, and others) worked in isolated studies, formulating hypotheses and mathematical derivations without access to permanent, modern experimental testing complexes.

A decisive turning point occurred in the late 1920s and early 1930s. Driven by the comprehensive industrialization of the Soviet state, isolated individual inquiries were rapidly replaced by **specialized scientific collectives organized and funded directly by the government**:

### 1. The Gas Dynamics Laboratory (GDL / Газодинамическая лаборатория)
* **Founding and Evolution**: Established in Moscow in 1921 by the distinguished chemist **Nikolay Ivanovich Tikhomirov (1860–1930)**, initially designated as the Laboratory for Inventions of the Military Department. It relocated to Leningrad in 1927 and was officially named the Gas Dynamics Laboratory (GDL) in 1928;
* **Foundations of Solid-Propellant Rocket Weaponry (Cradle of the Katyusha)**: Under the leadership of Tikhomirov, V. A. Artem'yev, B. S. Petropavlovskiy, and Georgiy Erikhovich Langemak, GDL conquered the critical problem of stable, high-pressure combustion of smokeless colloidal powder (double-base powder) in thin-walled combustion chambers. They developed a series of field rocket projectiles for aircraft takeoff assistance and air-to-ground combat—the direct technical ancestor of the famous World War II **"Katyusha" multiple rocket launcher (RS-82 and RS-132 airborne and ground-launched rockets)**!
* **Pioneering Liquid and Electric Rocket Engines (Glushko's Early Work)**: In May 1929, the young **Valentin Petrovich Glushko (1908–1989)** organized Section II at GDL, the first dedicated department in the USSR for the design and investigation of liquid-propellant and electric rocket engines. Glushko and his team developed the renowned **ORM series of liquid rocket engines** (from ORM-1 through ORM-65):
  * Experimentally verified bipropellant liquid systems using high-concentration **nitric acid**, nitrogen tetroxide, and liquid oxygen as oxidizers, and **kerosene** and toluene as fuels;
  * Pioneered **hypergolic chemical ignition**, **refractory ceramic thermal barrier coatings** on combustion chamber inner walls, and double-walled **regenerative cooling nozzles** operating on fluid pressure cycles;
  * Established an unshakeable engineering foundation for the future Soviet school of high-thrust liquid rocket engines.

### 2. The Group for the Study of Reactive Motion (GIRD / Группа изучения реактивного движения)
* **Founding Context**: In the autumn of 1931, under the auspices of the Society for the Promotion of Defense, Aviation, and Chemical Construction (Osoaviakhim), the Moscow Group for the Study of Reactive Motion (MosGIRD) and the Leningrad Group (LenGIRD) were established;
* **Leadership**: Moscow GIRD was directed jointly by F. A. Tsander and **Sergey Pavlovich Korolev (1907–1966)**, bringing together a brilliant cadre of energetic young talent including Yuriy Aleksandrovich Pobedonostsev and Mikhail Klavdiyevich Tikhonravov;
* **Historic Flight Milestones**:
  * **The Soviet Union's First Successful Liquid/Hybrid Rocket "GIRD-09"**: Designed under the direction of Tikhonravov, burning liquid oxygen and solidified gasoline-rosin gel, successfully launched on **August 17, 1933**, at Nakhabino outside Moscow, reaching an altitude of 400 meters;
  * **The Soviet Union's First Pure Liquid-Propellant Rocket "GIRD-X"**: Conceived by Tsander and completed after his death under the direction of Korolev, burning liquid oxygen and ethyl alcohol, successfully launched on **November 25, 1933**, reaching an altitude of 80 meters.

### 3. The Reaction Scientific Research Institute (RNII / Реактивный научно-исследовательский институт)
* **State Strategic Integration**: To eliminate duplication and consolidate scientific capabilities, the Soviet leadership, acting upon the direct initiative of M. N. Tukhachevskiy (Deputy Chairman of the Revolutionary Military Council and Deputy People's Commissar for Heavy Industry), made the historic decision in the **autumn of 1933** to **merge Leningrad GDL and Moscow GIRD into the world's first comprehensive state rocket research institution—the Reaction Scientific Research Institute (RNII / Scientific Research Institute No. 3)**;
* **Leadership Structure**: Ivan Terent'yevich Kleymenov was appointed Director, with G. E. Langemak as Deputy Director and Chief Engineer, and S. P. Korolev as Deputy Director and Head of Winged Rocket Vehicles;
* **Historical Achievements**: RNII unified GDL's profound expertise in solid propellants with GIRD's advanced work in aerodynamic vehicle configurations and liquid propulsion control:
  * Standardized and deployed the RS-82 and RS-132 solid aviation rockets and the legendary BM-13 "Katyusha" multiple rocket launcher system;
  * Developed the Soviet Union's first manned rocket-powered aircraft, the **RP-318**;
  * Educated and forged an entire generation of Soviet space commanders, including Korolev, Glushko, Tikhonravov, and Pobedonostsev.

It was precisely the tireless pioneering efforts of these early professional institutions that transformed the theoretical sketches of Russian pioneers into the epochal triumph of Sputnik-1 in 1957 and Yuriy Gagarin's historic leap into the cosmos in 1961!
