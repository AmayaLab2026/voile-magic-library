# Section 2: Tsander, Winged Spacecraft, Metallic Combustion, and Early Liquid Rocket Practice

In transforming Tsiolkovskiy's astronautical concepts into engineering realization and ground testing, an exalted historical position is occupied by the distinguished engineer, physicist, and inventor **Fridrikh Arturovich Tsander (1887–1933)**.

---

## I. Academic Biography and Three Core Original Concepts of Astronautical Theory

Tsander was born in Riga, Latvia, and graduated with distinction from the Mechanical Department of the Riga Polytechnic Institute in 1914. As early as his student years in 1908, inspired by Tsiolkovskiy's writings, he began keeping research notebooks and diaries dedicated to mathematical and engineering calculations of interplanetary flight. Following the outbreak of World War I, he was evacuated with his factory to Moscow, serving as a design engineer at the famous "Motor" aviation engine plant (1919–1926), during which time he devoted all his spare hours to astronautical exploration.

Tsander made three world-class, profoundly original contributions to astronautical dynamics and spacecraft engineering:

### 1. The Combined Winged Spacecraft Concept (Synthesis of Airplane and Rocket)
Whereas Tsiolkovskiy primarily envisioned vertically launched ballistic rockets, Tsander pioneered the concept of an **organic fusion of conventional aerodynamic aircraft and supersonic rocket propulsion**:
* **Atmospheric Takeoff and Acceleration Phase**: The vehicle utilizes aerodynamic lift from aircraft wings to support its gross weight, employing efficient aviation piston or air-breathing engines driving propellers to execute rolling takeoffs from runways and economical climbs through the dense atmosphere, thereby eliminating the massive "gravitational penalty" imposed on vertical rocket liftoffs;
* **Exoatmospheric Interplanetary Acceleration Phase**: Upon reaching high-altitude rarefied layers and maximum cruising airspeed, high-thrust rocket engines are ignited, accelerating the vehicle out of Earth's atmosphere and injecting it into interplanetary space;
* **Planetary Return Phase**: Returning to Earth, the spacecraft once again uses winged lifting surfaces to execute unpowered or powered hypersonic glides through the atmosphere, safely dissipating kinetic energy via aerodynamic drag before making conventional runway landings.

This visionary concept represents the direct theoretical and engineering ancestor of the modern Space Shuttle and aerospace planes!

### 2. Combustion of Jettisoned Structural Metal Components as High-Energy Fuel
To solve the severe mass-ratio limitations of chemical rockets, Tsander formulated the unique **"self-consuming rocket" principle**:
* During atmospheric ascent, a rocket requires large wings, external fuel tanks, landing gear, and heavy structural trusses; once in outer space, however, these aerodynamic elements become useless parasitic deadweight;
* Tsander proposed: manufacturing all structural components not needed in space (wings, struts, empty metal tanks) from combustible lightweight metals or alloys (such as **metallic aluminum, magnesium, or their alloys**);
* Once outside the atmosphere, mechanical devices would draw these discarded structural components into an onboard smelting crucible to melt them into liquid metal or pulverize them into ultrafine powders, injecting them directly into the rocket combustion chamber to react violently with liquid oxygen;
* Because metals such as aluminum and magnesium release tremendous heat when burned in pure oxygen (their volumetric and gravimetric heats of combustion vastly exceeding those of conventional hydrocarbons), utilizing discarded airframe structures as secondary high-energy fuel not only eliminates deadweight, but drastically multiplies the rocket's available propulsive energy!

### 3. Solar Radiation Pressure Sails and Fuel-Free Interplanetary Cruise
Tsander was among the earliest pioneers in the world to systematically calculate **deep-space cruising propelled by solar radiation pressure (Solar Sails)**. He mathematically derived the continuous thrust imparted by photon collisions against ultrathin, highly reflective metallic films of micron thickness, proving that once injected into interplanetary trajectories, spacecraft could cruise between planets of the solar system without consuming propellant, propelled entirely by solar sails spanning hundreds of thousands of square meters.

## II. From Theoretical Exploration to Ground Test Firing

In 1924, Tsander co-founded the Soviet Union's first public astronautical society—the **Society for Studies of Interplanetary Travel (OIMS)**—and published his seminal paper *Flights to Other Planets* (*Перелеты на другие планеты*) in issue No. 13 of *Tekhnika i Zhizn'* (*Technology and Life*).

Beginning in 1928, Tsander shifted his primary energies from pure theory to rigorous laboratory engineering, inaugurating the golden dawn of early Soviet liquid rocket testing:

### 1. The OR-1 Experimental Thermal Jet Engine (1929–1930)
Using a standard aviation blowtorch as a foundation, Tsander constructed the Soviet Union's first thermal jet test engine, the OR-1:
* Operating on compressed air as oxidizer and gasoline as fuel;
* Featuring an innovative preheating injector for powdered metallic magnesium inside the combustion chamber, systematically validating the feasibility of injecting and burning powdered metals within a hydrocarbon-air combustion stream;
* Executing dozens of static firings to measure thrust, combustion temperatures, and nozzle efficiency, gathering foundational experimental data for high-pressure all-liquid engines.

### 2. The OR-2 Liquid Rocket Engine and RP-1 Rocket Glider (1931–1933)
In 1931, supported by the Society for the Promotion of Defense, Aviation, and Chemical Construction (Osoaviakhim), Tsander, as chief technical leader alongside the young Sergey Pavlovich Korolev (future Chief Designer of the Soviet space program), co-founded the legendary **Group for the Study of Reactive Motion (GIRD)**:
* Tsander directed GIRD Brigade No. 1, dedicated to developing the **OR-2 liquid-propellant rocket engine**;
* Designed to burn **liquid oxygen** and **gasoline**, featuring water-cooled double-walled jacket regenerative cooling, producing a rated sea-level thrust of 50 kg ($500\ \text{N}$);
* Intended for installation in the tailless BICh-11 experimental glider designed by Korolev, forming the **RP-1**, the Soviet Union's first manned rocket-powered aircraft.

### 3. Genesis of the GIRD-X Liquid Rocket and Tsander's Enduring Legacy
While perfecting the OR-2, Tsander designed the more advanced and compact **GIRD-X liquid rocket engine** (burning liquid oxygen and ethyl alcohol, developing 70 kg of thrust).

However, years of exhausting, round-the-clock labor shattered Tsander's health. In early 1933, while en route to a sanatorium in Kislovodsk, North Caucasus, he contracted severe typhoid fever. On March 28, 1933, this great pioneer passed away prematurely at the age of 46.

Following Tsander's death, the GIRD collective, galvanized under Korolev's leadership, pushed through the final assembly and testing of the GIRD-X rocket. On November 25, 1933, at the Nakhabino testing grounds outside Moscow, **the Soviet Union's first liquid-propellant rocket, GIRD-X, roared into the sky!**

Tsander's lifelong rallying cry—**“Forward, to Mars!” (*Вперед, на Марс!*)**—was deeply etched into the hearts of Soviet space pioneers, becoming an eternal source of inspiration driving astronautics from the laboratory bench to the stars.
