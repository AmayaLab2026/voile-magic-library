## Propellant Combustion Mechanics and the Checkerboard Injector Array

Hydrogen and oxygen stored in solid or deep cryogenic liquid states must absorb heat to vaporize or sublimate prior to injection into the combustion chamber. This heat of vaporization is supplied by high-temperature gaseous hydrogen circulating through a regenerative cooling jacket surrounding the thrust tube and routed through heat-exchanger coils inside the propellant tanks. The vaporized, high-pressure gases are boosted by propellant feed pumps and then injected into the combustion zone.

For the combustion of liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen, three engineering approaches are conceivable:
1. **Premixed Combustion**: Thoroughly premixing the two propellants before entering the combustion chamber, followed by ignition within the hot chamber;
2. **Instantaneous Diffusion Combustion**: Delivering the two gases through entirely isolated plumbing until the physical instant of injection into the combustion zone;
3. **Partial Premixing**: Allowing a shallow degree of microscopic mixing within very short tubes immediately upstream of the injection orifices, followed instantly by discharge and ignition.

Which of these three methods is superior must ultimately be resolved by future ground-based hot-fire test stands (see Note 14 at the end of the text).

The decisive advantage of the first premixed approach is that it ensures complete stoichiometric combination of hydrogen and oxygen at the molecular scale, leaving zero unreacted residual gas. Its fatal hazard, however, is **severe flashback explosion**—the flame front easily detonates backward through the highly volatile mixture into the upstream mixing manifold. This flashback danger might be mitigated by borrowing from the Davy lamp principle used in mining safety lamps, interposing fine metal wire meshes or porous refractory matrices immediately upstream of the combustion zone to quench flame propagation. If proven viable in practical testing, premixed combustion should be the preferred choice.

Should the threat of flashback explosion prove insurmountable, one must fall back on partial premixing or completely separated injection. To achieve maximum gaseous mixing uniformity without premature detonation, Kondratyuk proposed an exceptionally advanced **checkerboard multi-orifice injector array**:

The liquid oxygen and liquid hydrogen manifolds at the injector faceplate are subdivided into thousands of dense, minute square nozzles whose orifices interlock in a two-dimensional grid, alternating in a pattern identical to an international chessboard (see Note 15 at the end of the text):

| Oxygen | Hydrogen | Oxygen | Hydrogen |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Hydrogen** | **Oxygen** | **Hydrogen** | **Oxygen** |
| **Oxygen** | **Hydrogen** | **Oxygen** | **Hydrogen** |
| **Hydrogen** | **Oxygen** | **Hydrogen** | **Oxygen** |

Through this exquisitely ingenious checkerboard geometry, even though both working fluids remain strictly separated prior to discharge, they instantly interlace upon injection into the chamber, forming ultra-fine alternating micro-layers that achieve exceptionally rapid, homogeneous turbulent mixing and deflagration within a minimal combustion chamber length.

## Optimum Geometric Profile for Expansion Thrust Tubes (Laval Nozzles)

To convert the thermal energy of high-temperature, high-pressure combustion gases into directed kinetic exhaust energy with maximum efficiency, the geometric contouring of the expansion thrust tube is of paramount importance.

![Parabolic thrust expansion nozzle cross-section (Figure 11)](/images/rocket-pioneers/build-fig11.png)  
*Figure 11: Optimum longitudinal geometric profile of the thrust tube (nozzle)*

Figure 11 illustrates the optimum longitudinal profile of the expansion nozzle calculated by Kondratyuk:
* The overall profile closely approximates a **paraboloid of revolution**, with a slight flaring correction near the convergent entrance;
* At the nozzle throat (the minimum cross-section between the combustion chamber and expansion duct), the flow area contracts sharply, accelerating the gas to local sonic velocity;
* Downstream along the long, smooth parabolic bell expansion contour, supersonic gas expands smoothly and adiabatically against the nozzle wall, with pressure dropping from tens of thousands of absolute units to ambient back-pressure, converting internal static energy into colossal axial supersonic kinetic energy.

In the vacuum of space, where ambient back-pressure is zero, achieving peak specific impulse requires expanding the nozzle exit area as wide as possible; during liftoff through Earth's dense lower atmosphere, however, an excessively large expansion exit area would cause severe flow overexpansion and boundary-layer separation, potentially triggering destructive nozzle wall flutter and severe localized thermal degradation. Therefore, during atmospheric boost, the nozzle exit could be temporarily fitted with a detachable throttling sleeve that narrows the effective exit area, to be jettisoned immediately upon reaching the rarefied upper atmosphere.

## Celestial Optical Goniometer

In the dark void of space, devoid of landmarks or horizons, gyroscopic inertial references accumulate drift over time. The spacecraft must therefore possess precision optical sighting instruments capable of observing external stars and celestial bodies to recalibrate its absolute coordinates and heading in space.

![Celestial sighting periscopic goniometer (Figure 12)](/images/rocket-pioneers/build-fig12.png)  
*Figure 12: Optical path of the celestial sighting prism periscope*

Figure 12 displays the celestial sighting goniometer designed by Kondratyuk:
* The system features an optical periscope mounted in a sealed two-degree-of-freedom ball-and-socket joint in the cabin hull;
* Utilizing precision prism mirrors, the cosmonaut can perform a **panoramic $360^{\circ}$ celestial sweep** from within the cabin eyepiece without exposing any part of the body to the vacuum;
* Calibrated dials read the spatial angles between the spacecraft's reference axis and two benchmark celestial bodies (such as the Sun and Polaris), providing an absolute celestial reference frame for trajectory correction and engine burns.

## "Eureka!"—The Great Discovery of Planetary Atmospheric Aerodynamic Braking

As he arrived at this point in the manuscript, Kondratyuk experienced the most legendary epiphany of his life—one destined to exert a profound and lasting impact on world astronautics. In the margin of the manuscript, he excitedly scrawled the famous Greek exclamation: **"Eureka!" (I have found it!)**.

Prior to this moment, all computational models of returning to Earth from interplanetary space—including the concepts of the earliest pioneers—had pessimistically assumed: **a spacecraft returning to Earth must consume a quantity of propellant comparable to that expended during departure, relying on rocket retro-firing against its trajectory to cancel tens of Machs of cosmic velocity down to zero!**

Were that the case, the rocket mass ratio exponent at launch would have to be squared ($e^{2\sqrt{\frac{rj}{p}}}$), subjecting the initial liftoff mass to an astronomical, exponential explosion that rendered round-trip manned interplanetary flight virtually impossible from an engineering perspective.

**Yet at this instant, Kondratyuk saw with blinding clarity the priceless gift of the planetary atmosphere!**

During ascent away from Earth, the dense atmosphere is undeniably a hazardous obstacle, imposing friction drag, aerodynamic heating, and severe dynamic pressure loads;  
**During return to Earth, however, this very atmosphere is the most perfect natural, fuel-free super-brake imaginable!**

Consider: when the spacecraft returns from deep space, there is absolutely no need to fire the engines and burn precious fuel! The vehicle need only enter the atmosphere along a **grazing, tangential entry corridor** at a shallow flight-path angle:
* Rather than plunging vertically into the lower atmosphere, the spacecraft glides through the rarefied upper atmospheric layers hundreds of kilometers above the surface;
* Relying upon the continuous aerodynamic drag of the thin upper atmosphere, the enormous orbital kinetic energy of tens of thousands of kilometers per hour is smoothly dissipated and converted into ambient atmospheric shockwave heat along an extended gliding trajectory;
* As velocity is gradually bled off, the spacecraft naturally decays into progressively lower orbital altitudes, eventually settling smoothly and safely to the ground!

This third return method—utilizing atmospheric aerodynamic braking—**halves the crushing return propellant exponent in the mass equation**:
$$M = m \cdot e^{\sqrt{\frac{rj}{p}}}$$

The required initial liftoff mass of the rocket, which would otherwise have exploded quadratically, was slashed by an entire order of magnitude in a single stroke! **It was this "Eureka" discovery that definitively established the physical and engineering feasibility of human manned spaceflight and interplanetary return!**
