## Appendix I: Public Law 91-513 — The Comprehensive Drug Abuse Prevention and Control Act of 1970
*(Historical Comparison: Enactment of October 27, 1970 vs. Code of Federal Regulations, 21 CFR § 1308, as of April 1, 1990)*

Dr. Shulgin provided a detailed comparative analysis contrasting the **initial statutory schedules enacted on October 27, 1970** against the **codified regulatory schedules of the Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR § 1308) revised as of April 1, 1990**.

### I. Schedule I Statutory Evolution

#### (a) Synthetic Opiates and Opioid Derivatives
* **Statutory Criteria**: High potential for abuse; no currently accepted medical use in treatment in the United States; lack of accepted safety under medical supervision.
* **Original 1970 Enactment (42 substances)**: Included acetylmethadol, allylprodine, alphamethadol, benzylmorphine, phenoperidine, dextromoramide, dimepheptanol, early fentanyl analogues, and levomoramide.
* **1990 Codified Amendments**:
  * **Proliferation of Designer Fentanyls**: Via successive *Federal Register* emergency orders, added **alpha-methylfentanyl (9814)**, **3-methylfentanyl (9813)**, **para-fluorofentanyl (9812)**, **acetyl-alpha-methylfentanyl (9815)**, and **thiofentanyl (9835)**;
  * **Meperidine Reversed-Ester Analogues**: Added **MPPP (1-methyl-4-phenyl-4-propionoxypiperidine, 9661)** and its parkinsonism-inducing contaminant **PEPAP (1-(2-phenylethyl)-4-phenyl-4-acetyloxypiperidine, 9663)**;
  * **Novel Opioid Agonists**: Added dihydroetorphine, tilidine, and carfentanil intermediates.

#### (b) Natural Opium Derivatives
* **Original 1970 Enactment (22 substances)**: Heroin (9200), acetyldihydrocodeine, benzylmorphine, desomorphine, dihydromorphine, nicomorphine, normorphine, and related alkaloids.
* **1990 Statutory Status**: Substantively stable, with statutory preambles explicitly encompassing all salts, esters, ethers, and isomers whenever their existence is possible.

#### (c) Hallucinogenic Substances
* **Original 1970 Enactment (17 substances)**: 3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine (MDA), 5-methoxy-3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine (MMDA), 3,4,5-trimethoxyamphetamine (TMA), bufotenine, diethyltryptamine (DET), dimethyltryptamine (DMT), 4-methyl-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine (DOM / STP), ibogaine, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), marihuana, mescaline, peyote, psilocybin, psilocyn, and tetrahydrocannabinols (THC).
* **1990 Massive Statutory Expansion**:
  * **Comprehensive Closure of Designer Phenethylamines**: Added **MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine, 7405)**, **DOB (4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine)**, **PMA (para-methoxyamphetamine)**, **2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine (2,5-DMA)**, and related positional isomers;
  * **Synthetic Tryptamines**: Added **5-MeO-DMT** and diisopropyltryptamine homologues;
  * **Phencyclidine (PCP) Analogues**: Added **PCE (N-ethyl-1-phenylcyclohexylamine, 7455)**, **PCPr (1-(1-phenylcyclohexyl)pyrrolidine, 7458)**, **TCP (1-[1-(2-thienyl)cyclohexyl]piperidine, 7470)**, and **TCPy (7473)**;
  * **Statutory Definition of Isomers**: Explicitly expanded to encompass "**any optical, positional, and geometric isomers**."

#### (d) Depressants and Stimulants
* **Depressants**: Methaqualone (2565, transferred by special Act of Congress from Schedule II to Schedule I in 1984) and mecloqualone (2572).
* **Stimulants**: Added fenethylline (1503), N-ethylamphetamine (1475), cathinone (1235), and methcathinone.

---

### II. Schedule II Statutory Evolution

* **Statutory Criteria**: High potential for abuse; currently accepted medical use in treatment in the United States (or accepted medical use with severe restrictions); abuse may lead to severe psychic or physical dependence.
* **Core Controlled Categories**:
  1. **Natural and Semisynthetic Opioids**: Raw opium, powdered opium, morphine (9300), codeine (9050), hydrocodone (9193), oxycodone (9143), oxymorphone, and thebaine (9333);
  2. **Coca Leaves and Cocaine**: Coca leaf extracts, cocaine (9041), its salts, and ecgonine derivatives;
  3. **Synthetic Opioids**: Fentanyl (9801), methadone (9250), meperidine (9230), sufentanil (9740), alfentanil (9737), levorphanol, and alphaprodine;
  4. **Central Nervous System Stimulants (Transferred from Schedule III in 1971)**: Amphetamine (1100), methamphetamine (1105), phenmetrazine, and methylphenidate (Ritalin, 1724);
  5. **Short- and Intermediate-Acting Barbiturates**: Amobarbital (2125), pentobarbital (2270), and secobarbital (2315);
  6. **Immediate Chemical Precursors**: Phenylacetone (P2P, 8501), 1-phenylcyclohexylamine, and 1-piperidinocyclohexanecarbonitrile (PCC, PCP precursor).

---

### III. Schedules III, IV, and V Statutory Evolution

* **Schedule III**:
  * Intermediate-acting barbiturates (e.g., butabarbital, talbutal);
  * Opioid combination formulations with non-controlled analgesics ($\le 1.8\text{ g}$ codeine per $100\text{ mL}$, or $\le 90\text{ mg}$ per dosage unit);
  * **1990 Landmark Addition**: Comprehensive scheduling of twenty-seven **Anabolic Steroids**, including trenbolone, stanozolol, nandrolone, testosterone, and their esters.
* **Schedule IV**:
  * **Comprehensive Coverage of Benzodiazepines**: Over thirty clinical anxiolytics and hypnotics, including diazepam, alprazolam, lorazepam, triazolam, clonazepam, and flurazepam;
  * Long-acting barbiturates: Phenobarbital (2285) and mephobarbital;
  * Appetite suppressants and mild stimulants/analgesics: Dextropropoxyphene (Darvon), mazindol, and pentazocine.
* **Schedule V**:
  * Low-concentration codeine antitussive syrups ($\le 200\text{ mg}$ codeine per $100\text{ mL}$);
  * Low-dose diphenoxylate antidiarrheal preparations compounded with atropine ($\le 2.5\text{ mg}$ diphenoxylate per dosage unit).

## Appendix II: Public Law 98-473 — Emergency Scheduling Authority
*(Comprehensive Crime Control Act of 1984, 21 U.S.C. § 811(h))*

Enacted on October 12, 1984, the Comprehensive Crime Control Act of 1984 amended Section 201 of the Controlled Substances Act by adding **Subsection (h): Temporary Scheduling to Avoid an Imminent Hazard to the Public Safety**.

### Statutory Mechanisms and Scope of Authority
1. **Statutory Standard**: The Attorney General (delegated to the Administrator of the DEA) determines that placing an uncontrolled substance temporarily into Schedule I is necessary to **avoid an imminent hazard to the public safety**.
2. **Statutory Factors to Consider**: In making this finding, the Attorney General is required to evaluate only three statutory criteria:
   * (A) The history and current pattern of abuse of the substance;
   * (B) The scope, duration, and significance of such abuse;
   * (C) What risk, if any, there is to the public health, including acute toxic or lethal liability.
3. **Procedural Exemptions**: The statute **expressly exempts** emergency scheduling orders from the formal notice-and-comment rulemaking procedures of the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), judicial review, and the requirement of awaiting a formal scientific and medical evaluation from the Secretary of Health and Human Services (HHS).
4. **Advance Notice and Duration**:
   * The Attorney General must provide written notice to the Secretary of HHS at least **30 days** prior to publishing a temporary scheduling order in the *Federal Register*;
   * A temporary scheduling order remains in effect for **one year**, with an optional **six-month extension** if permanent rulemaking proceedings have been formally initiated (maximum statutory duration of 18 months).

## Appendix III: Public Law 99-570 — Controlled Substance Analogue Enforcement Act of 1986
*(21 U.S.C. § 802(32) & § 813)*

Enacted on October 27, 1986, the Controlled Substance Analogue Enforcement Act was designed to eliminate the loophole exploited by clandestine laboratories synthesizing unlisted chemical congeners to evade the scheduling lists.

### Statutory Provisions
```text
§ 813. Treatment of controlled substance analogues
A controlled substance analogue shall, to the extent intended for human consumption,
be treated, for the purposes of any Federal law, as a controlled substance in Schedule I.

§ 802(32)(A). Definition of controlled substance analogue
Except as provided in subparagraph (C), the term "controlled substance analogue" means a substance—
(i) the chemical structure of which is substantially similar to the chemical structure
    of a controlled substance in schedule I or II;
(ii) which has a stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic effect on the central nervous system
     that is substantially similar to or greater than the stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic
     effect on the central nervous system of a controlled substance in schedule I or II; or
(iii) with respect to a particular person, which such person represents or intends to have a
      stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic effect on the central nervous system that is
      substantially similar to or greater than the stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic effect
      on the central nervous system of a controlled substance in schedule I or II.

(C). Exclusions
Such term does not include—
(i) a controlled substance;
(ii) any substance for which there is an approved new drug application under section 505
     of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act;
(iii) with respect to a particular person, any substance, if an exemption is in effect
      for investigational use, for that person, under section 505 of the Federal Food,
      Drug, and Cosmetic Act to the extent conduct with respect to such substance is
      pursuant to such exemption; or
(iv) any substance to the extent not intended for human consumption.
```

## Appendix IV: Federal Sentencing Guidelines and Drug Equivalency Tables
*(U.S.S.G. § 2D1.1)*

Pursuant to the Sentencing Reform Act of 1984, the United States Sentencing Commission established standardized **Base Offense Levels (Levels 6 through 42)** for federal drug trafficking offenses based upon net drug quantities.

### I. Base Offense Levels and Drug Quantities Matrix

| Base Offense Level | Net Heroin | Net Cocaine | Pure Methamphetamine | Marihuana Equivalent | Federal Sentencing Guideline Range |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Level 38** | $\ge 10\text{ kg}$ | $\ge 50\text{ kg}$ | $\ge 5\text{ kg}$ | $\ge 10,000\text{ kg}$ | **292 — 365 months (24–30 years)** |
| **Level 36** | $3 - 10\text{ kg}$ | $15 - 50\text{ kg}$ | $1.5 - 5\text{ kg}$ | $3,000 - 10,000\text{ kg}$ | **188 — 235 months (15–20 years)** |
| **Level 34** | $1 - 3\text{ kg}$ | $5 - 15\text{ kg}$ | $500\text{ g} - 1.5\text{ kg}$ | $1,000 - 3,000\text{ kg}$ | **151 — 188 months (12–15 years)** |
| **Level 32** | $700\text{ g} - 1\text{ kg}$ | $3.5 - 5\text{ kg}$ | $350 - 500\text{ g}$ | $700 - 1,000\text{ kg}$ | **121 — 151 months (10–12 years)** |
| **Level 28** | $100 - 400\text{ g}$ | $500\text{ g} - 2\text{ kg}$ | $50 - 200\text{ g}$ | $100 - 400\text{ kg}$ | **78 — 97 months (6.5–8 years)** |
| **Level 26** | $60 - 100\text{ g}$ | $300 - 500\text{ g}$ | $30 - 50\text{ g}$ | $60 - 100\text{ kg}$ | **63 — 78 months (5–6.5 years)** |
| **Level 20** | $10 - 20\text{ g}$ | $50 - 100\text{ g}$ | $5 - 10\text{ g}$ | $10 - 20\text{ kg}$ | **33 — 41 months (~3 years)** |
| **Level 12** | $< 5\text{ g}$ | $< 25\text{ g}$ | $< 2.5\text{ g}$ | $< 5\text{ kg}$ | **10 — 16 months (~1 year)** |

### II. Drug Equivalency Table
To compute aggregate offense levels involving diverse controlled substances, the sentencing guidelines establish standardized conversions expressed in grams of marihuana equivalency:
* $1\text{ g}$ **Heroin** $= 1,000\text{ g}$ Marihuana ($1\text{ kg}$)
* $1\text{ g}$ **Cocaine** $= 200\text{ g}$ Marihuana
* $1\text{ g}$ **Pure Methamphetamine** $= 10,000\text{ g}$ Marihuana ($10\text{ kg}$)
* $1\text{ g}$ **Fentanyl (pure)** $= 2,500\text{ g}$ Marihuana
* $1\text{ g}$ **3-Methylfentanyl** $= 125,000\text{ g}$ Marihuana ($125\text{ kg}$)
* $1\text{ g}$ **LSD (pure crystalline)** $= 100,000\text{ g}$ Marihuana ($100\text{ kg}$)
* $1\text{ g}$ **MDMA / MDA** $= 50\text{ g}$ Marihuana

## Appendix V: Public Law 100-690 — Laws and Regulations Concerning Precursors
*(Chemical Diversion and Trafficking Act of 1988, 21 U.S.C. § 802 & 21 CFR § 1310)*

Enacted under Subtitle A of Title VI of the Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1988, the Chemical Diversion and Trafficking Act (CDTA) instituted federal regulatory oversight over precursor and essential chemicals used in illicit drug synthesis.

### I. List I Chemicals (Precursors)
Chemicals directly incorporated into the molecular structure of controlled substances during clandestine manufacture, subject to mandatory transaction recordkeeping, DEA registration, and export/import quotas:

| List I Chemical | Illicit Target Compound | Statutory Reporting Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Ephedrine** | Methamphetamine, Methcathinone | **Any quantity (Zero threshold)** |
| **Pseudoephedrine** | Methamphetamine, Desoxyephedrine | $1\text{ kg}$ |
| **Phenylpropanolamine (PPA)** | Amphetamine | $2.5\text{ kg}$ |
| **Phenylacetic acid** | Phenylacetone (P2P), Methamphetamine | $1\text{ kg}$ |
| **Benzyl cyanide** | Phenylacetone (P2P), Amphetamines | $1\text{ kg}$ |
| **Piperidine** | Phencyclidine (PCP) and analogues | $500\text{ g}$ |
| **N-Acetylanthranilic acid** | Methaqualone | $40\text{ kg}$ |
| **Anthranilic acid** | Methaqualone, Mecloqualone | $30\text{ kg}$ |
| **Ephedra (Ma Huang)** | Natural ephedrine source extract | Any commercial distribution |

### II. List II Chemicals (Essential Reagents & Solvents)
Organic solvents, reagents, and catalytic acids/bases essential to the synthesis, extraction, or purification of controlled substances:

| List II Chemical | Principal Illicit Application | Statutory Reporting Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Ethyl ether** | Cocaine freebase conversion, Grignard reactions | $136\text{ kg}$ (~50 gallons) |
| **Acetone** | Heroin and cocaine purification, precursor synthesis | $150\text{ kg}$ |
| **2-Butanone (Methyl Ethyl Ketone / MEK)** | Methamphetamine and cocaine dehydration solvent | $145\text{ kg}$ |
| **Toluene** | P2P reaction solvent, alkaloid extraction | $159\text{ kg}$ |
| **Potassium permanganate** | Oxidation of coca paste impurities in cocaine refining | $500\text{ kg}$ |
| **Hydrogen chloride gas (anhydrous)** | Hydrochloride salt crystallization of cocaine and meth | Any industrial cylinder distribution |
| **Sulfuric acid** | Opium hydrolysis, amphetamine sulfate crystallization | Bulk tanker or barrel shipment |
